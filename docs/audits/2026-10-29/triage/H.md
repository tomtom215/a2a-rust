## 2. Decisions needed

The handoff says H5-H11 and H19 need an ADR and the maintainer's choice before they are built. Each block gives options, one recommendation, and what would prove it.

**D1 (H5b) Lease control-plane cost.**
A. A per-replica sweeper replaces the per-turn watchers: one expired-lease scan per second. Internal, no wire change, but `LeaseStore` needs a scan method and an owner column so replicas do not scan each other's rows.
B. Lease groups on the wire: one renewal call covers all children of a parent. Cuts RPCs, but it is a new extension version and only helps peers that implement it (H19).
C. Measure first: a `swarm_scale` arm that holds 10^3, 10^4, 5x10^4 idle leased turns per replica on `InMemoryLeaseStore` and Postgres and counts statements per second.
Recommend C, then A. Defer B until H19 is settled. Size: C S, A M. Proof: the arm's statements-per-second is flat in the number of leases after A; a counting `LeaseStore` wrapper test. Breaking: no (new default trait method). Theme: swarm-design.

**D2 (H6a, b, c) Retry budget, circuit breaker, in-flight cap.**
A. Per-client opt-in: `RetryPolicy::with_budget(ratio, min_per_sec)` token bucket inside `RetryTransport`, a per-endpoint consecutive-failure breaker as a `Transport` wrapper (the E3 shape), `ClientBuilder::with_max_in_flight(n)` returning the already retryable `TooManyPendingRequests`. State lives in the client, so 1000 clients still amplify.
B. Process-wide registry keyed by authority (needs the shared pool of H2's second half). Right answer for a supervisor with many `A2aClient`s, more to build.
C. Say retries should be off behind a mesh that has budgets and outlier ejection (Envoy), and document it.
Recommend A now, with `with_overall_deadline` (H6d) in the same PR, and B once H2's pool exists. Size M. Proof: E3's "failing first": the (N+1)th call to a closed port fails with no accept on the listener; 1000 calls against a 503 peer with a 10% budget make about 1,100 attempts, not 4,000. Breaking: no. Theme: client-resilience.

**D3 (H7a, b, c) Readiness, saturation and halt.**
A. Keep `/ready` as reachability plus draining (the documented choice) and export utilisation as a gauge (queue depth over `max_concurrent_queues`; the cap is not exported today) with an HPA or alert recipe.
B. Opt-in `ready_high_watermark` returning 503 `saturated`. Risk: every replica saturates together, the balancer answers 503 itself with no `Retry-After`, which is worse than the app's `Overloaded` plus `RetryInfo`.
C. Add `halted` and `saturated` as informational fields in the 200 body, status unchanged.
Recommend A plus C, S. A halted replica deliberately serves reads and cancels, so making it unready hides the operator's view. Proof: with cap 2 and two held tasks `/ready` is 200 and the body says `saturated`; the gauge reads 1.0. Breaking: no. Theme: observability.

**D4 (H9a) Admission: queue, priority, fairness.**
A. Per-tenant reservations plus a shared burst pool (guaranteed slots per tenant, borrow the rest). Fairness with no queue, no added latency or memory.
B. A bounded queue with `max_wait` capped by the caller's remaining budget, with DRR across tenants. Slots are held for a whole turn (seconds to hours), so a 500 ms queue helps only bursts of short turns.
C. Priority-aware shedding: priority from budget metadata, refuse low priority first above a high-water mark.
Recommend A plus C, M; B only if a measurement shows short-turn bursts dominate. Proof: swarm_scale arm, tenant A floods, tenant B's admit rate and p95 stay inside its reservation. Breaking: no. Theme: swarm-design.

**D5 (H10) Lineage and job-scoped halt.**
A. Lineage extension (`root`, `parent`, `depth`) in message metadata, attached by `Delegation` from the `RequestContext`; stored with the task; `HaltScope::Root(String)`; `max_depth`. A tree API cannot be a `ListTasks` filter without diverging from the generated proto, so it needs its own extension method or a store query.
B. Use `contextId` as the job id, propagated by `Delegation`: no new field, but a context is per conversation and server-generated when absent.
C. Tenant per job, today: works with tenant-aware stores, but a tenant is a storage and limits partition, heavy per job, and the tenant is not inherited by hand-written hops.
Recommend A, with `root` defaulting to the originating `contextId` so B is a special case; propose `parentTaskId` upstream later (swarm-orchestration G1-C). Size M-L. Proof: the two-root halt test in the table. Breaking: no. Theme: swarm-design.

**D6 (H11a) Tenant, agent and job dimension on metrics.**
A. Opt-in bounded attribute: top N tenants by volume plus `other`, N=0 by default.
B. No label; per-tenant spend goes to a usage ledger from budget settlement (audit or a hook), never to Prometheus.
C. Leave it to span-derived metrics (spans already carry `a2a.tenant`).
Recommend A on the refusal and admission counters only, and B for cost. Size S-M. Proof: 10,000 tenants produce at most N+1 series in a `ManualReader`. Breaking: no. Theme: observability.

**D7 (H12) Client load balancing, ejection, hedging.**
A. Decline in the core crates (the stated scope: no mesh) and document L4/L7 balancing, a headless service, and gRPC `dns:///` (the SDK's channel does not balance).
B. An `EndpointSet` `Transport` wrapper in a companion crate: power-of-two choices, passive ejection fed by D2's breaker. Hedging only for idempotent reads, or sends with an idempotency key.
C. In the core crates: contradicts the scope decision.
Recommend A now; B only after D2 ships and an adopter asks. Size A S, B M. Breaking: no. Theme: client-resilience.

**D8 (H13a, b) Byte budget and blob offload.**
A. Process-wide `max_buffered_bytes` over in-flight event bytes. Hard to account on `tokio::broadcast` (events are `Arc`-shared and freed when the last receiver reads), and it adds a refusal path.
B. Lower the defaults and publish a sizing formula (`queues x slots x max event`); leave offload to the agent, which can already emit `url` parts.
C. Status quo, container memory limit.
Recommend B now (S), and add a large-event RSS arm to `swarm_scale` (1k queues x 1 MiB events) before choosing A. For offload, keep it out of core until a measurement shows row size is the bottleneck: a `BlobStore` rewrites parts the client and ADR 0027 signatures see. Breaking: no. Theme: swarm-design.

**D9 (H14b) Push durability.**
A. Document best-effort per process and recommend a poll or subscribe backstop (S).
B. A `PushOutbox` table written with the status delta in one transaction, drained per replica with `SKIP LOCKED`, at-least-once with `seq` for the receiver to de-duplicate. Needs a store-trait method (default "unsupported") and PG/SQLite schema; the in-memory store keeps lanes.
C. A custom `PushSender` that enqueues to an external queue (possible today, `book/.../custom-sender.md`); it still loses the gap between the store commit and the sender call.
Recommend A now and B for Postgres behind a feature. Size M-L. Proof: the kill-after-commit test in the table. Breaking: no. Theme: push-delivery.

**D10 (H16c) Rate limiter capacity policy.**
A. Keep fail-closed, document it, warn at 80% full, label the refusal separately; optionally raise the default.
B. Overflow bucket: new callers share a stricter bucket when full (degrades instead of refusing; weakens the guard against an address-spraying caller).
C. Count-min sketch: bounded memory, never closed, over-counts.
Recommend A (S), B as an option behind a setter. Breaking: no. Theme: observability / docs.

**D11 (H19b) Polyglot swarm controls.**
A. Stand-alone spec page per extension with JSON Schema and language-neutral conformance vectors, checked by Rust tests and a small script in `itk/interop`.
B. Reference middleware for a2a-python and a2a-go, about 200 lines each; a maintenance burden tied to their APIs.
C. Upstream proposals: drafts exist for budget and provenance and are unsent; write lease (and lineage after D5).
D. A pyo3 wheel: large new release surface, against the project's "boringly correct" stance.
Recommend A then C, B only on adopter demand, skip D. Size A S-M, C S. Proof: the vectors run green against the Rust implementation and one Python reference (checked by reading the vectors, run in CI). Breaking: no. Theme: swarm-design / docs.

## 4. Notes for the maintainer
